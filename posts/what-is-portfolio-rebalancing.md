If you hold more than one cryptocurrency, your portfolio drifts. A coin that doubled now dominates your allocation; a coin that dipped shrank to almost nothing. Left unchecked, you end up with a portfolio that looks nothing like the one you planned.

Rebalancing is the fix: periodically selling what grew too large and buying what shrank too small, bringing every asset back to its target weight.

## Why rebalance at all?

Most investors pick target allocations for a reason — maybe 50% Bitcoin, 30% Ethereum, 20% everything else. Market moves push those numbers around daily. Without rebalancing you are, in effect, letting the market decide your strategy.

Regular rebalancing enforces a simple discipline: buy low, sell high. Not because you predicted the market, but because math requires it. When an asset's share grows past its target, you trim it. When it shrinks below target, you add to it.

## The problem with doing it manually

In theory, you could rebalance by hand. Check your portfolio, calculate the drift, place the trades. In practice, three things get in the way:

1. **Emotion.** It's hard to sell a coin that's pumping or buy one that's crashing, even when your strategy demands it.
2. **Time.** Checking allocations and calculating trade sizes across multiple exchanges gets tedious fast.
3. **Consistency.** Manual rebalancing tends to happen when you remember, not when it should.
